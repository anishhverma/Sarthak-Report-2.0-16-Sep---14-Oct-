02The verdict in seven answers

Q1Better or worse than 28 days ago?
Better per episode, smaller as a month. The three new Sunday Shows averaged +17% engaged views, +19% watch hours and +17% impressions against the six episodes before, and the typical episode gained +50% more subscribers. Viewers watched a smaller share of each episode (33.9% against 37.5%), and with 3 episodes instead of 6 the month’s long-form watch hours fell 39%.
Q2What improved?
Reach and subscribers per episode. Typical subscribers per lakh engaged views rose from 164 to 227. 4 Oct (3,217 subscribers, 340 per lakh) converted better than every episode in either period except 23 Aug.
Q3What got worse?
Attention. The share watched fell 3.6 points (2.3 against Sunday Shows only) as episodes got longer and the lead story grew from about 10% of runtime to 26%. 27 Sep ran below YouTube’s typical-retention band for most of its first 37 minutes. The first 30 seconds did not improve.
Q4Which previous changes worked?
One, directionally: the subscribe ask inside the show. 4 Oct put two subscribe jokes at about 23 minutes and converted 340 per lakh, double the 170 median of Sundays with no ask before 30:00. The new cold openings did not help.
Q5Which hypotheses were wrong?
The fix that followed the first-30-seconds diagnosis. Replacing the greeting with a cold open held 62–63% at 0:30; the host-led openings that named the week’s fight within 10 seconds held 66–70%. Also too broad: “oversized chapters hurt”. 11 Oct’s 11:42 lead ran above typical; only 27 Sep’s 13:19 tragedy block ran under.
Q6The new #1 bottleneck
Attention in the first 30 seconds and in the lead block. Every episode loses 34–38% of viewers by 0:30, and the lead decides where the rest of the episode sits against typical. The writers’ doc shows why leads run long: 27 Sep’s two biggest blocks were each planned at 2.5 minutes and ran 13:19 and 9:39 after stories were merged into them.
Q7Video to study
4 Oct, CJP vs Gyanesh Kumar. It named the fight at 0:09 (66% at 0:30), held the typical line for 40 minutes, spiked on absurd items (the Indra Dev complaint at 12:33, the monkey-and-vodka clip at 27:49), ran two subscribe jokes with no dip, and added 3,217 subscribers.

07Three experiments for the next 28 days

Ordered by expected effect on growth. They change different minutes of the same episode (the first 30 seconds, two points in the body, the lead block), so all three can run on the same Sundays. Hold runtime at or under 45 minutes while they run. When YouTube’s video A/B testing reaches the channel, run Experiment 1 as an A/B test of two cuts.

Experiment 1

Host-led conflict hook inside 15 seconds

Hypothesis. Retention at 0:30 depends on how fast the host names this week’s fight, not on whether the show greets the viewer. Openings that did this inside 10 seconds held 66–70%; openings that began with mood held 62–63%.

Production change
Greeting and tagline in 8 seconds or less, as now. By 0:15 the host names the week’s fight in one line with a hook or a joke, as 4 Oct did (“Gyanesh Kumar urf Gyaanu”, then the song joke). Write that line into the rundown’s “Hook Line” column for the lead. The first concrete fact lands by 0:30. Cold-open material (songs, monologues, vox pops, on-ground raps) moves to after the first fact. On tragedy weeks the hook is the fact itself, said plainly.
Control
After cohort 62%, 66%, 63% (mean 63.7%). Sunday history 64%. Best recent: 6 Sep at 70%.
Primary metric
Studio key moments: still watching at 0:30.
Guardrail
At 1:00 and 2:00 the curve stays at or above Studio’s typical band (no cliff moved later). First-24h Studio views at or above the new cohort’s floor of about 11.0L.
Minimum sample
4 consecutive Sundays.
Success threshold
66% or more on at least 3 of 4.
If it wins
It becomes the script rule for every Sunday. Cold-open ideas move into the body as bits, where 4 Oct’s spikes show they work.
If it loses
The opening is not the lever and 0:30 follows the topic. Stop iterating openings and put the effort into the lead block (Experiment 3).
Experiment 2

Two subscribe jokes inside stories, before 30:00

Hypothesis. Conversion follows where the ask lands. An ask around 23 minutes reaches about twice as many viewers as the sign-off. Sundays with an ask before 30:00 converted at a median 340 per 100K, against 170 without, and the gap holds within CJP weeks (528 against 202) and other weeks (228 against 121).

Production change
Two subscribe jokes written into stories, the first between 10:00 and 15:00 and the second between 20:00 and 25:00, in the 4 Oct style: inside a bit, in the host’s voice, no break in the story. Mark both as fixed beats in the script so they survive the shoot and the edit (27 Sep’s scripted ask did not). Keep the sign-off ask.
Control
No ask before 30:00: 6 Sep 178, 13 Sep 79, 27 Sep 162, 11 Oct 227 per 100K engaged (median 170). With one: 23 Aug 716, 30 Aug 228, 4 Oct 340.
Primary metric
Net subscribers per 100K engaged views at day 10.
Guardrail
No dip under Studio’s typical band within 30 seconds of either ask (4 Oct showed none). APV within 1 point of the cohort median.
Minimum sample
4 Sundays, at least 2 of them not led by the CJP storyline.
Success threshold
Median of 250 or more, and 200 or more on the non-CJP weeks.
If it wins
A standing script beat with a named owner in the writers’ room.
If it loses
Conversion is a topic effect, not a placement effect. Stop spending script time on asks and invest in storyline continuity instead.
Experiment 3

Tragedy weeks: one case, six minutes, then a release

Hypothesis. A long, stacked tragedy block is what pushes a Sunday under typical, not lead length in general. 27 Sep’s 13:19 block ran 6–7 points under and the episode never recovered; 11 Oct’s equally long CJP lead ran 5–9 points above; 30 Aug’s 3:11 tragedy lead kept APV at 36.3%.

Production change
When the week’s top story is a tragedy, cover one case in six minutes or less, with the accountability angle, then cut to an absurd or light item before any second heavy story. Put the six minutes in the rundown and do not merge further cases into the lead after Friday. Related cases move later or into a one-line round-up. The open still follows Experiment 1.
Control
27 Sep: lead about 5 points under typical on average, APV 31.7%, 162 per 100K. 13 Sep (11:49 tragedy lead): APV 33.2%, 79 per 100K.
Primary metric
Position against Studio’s typical band at 10:00, and APV at day 10.
Guardrail
First-24h views within the new cohort’s 11.0–12.3L, so the story is still covered and packaged in full. At least 170 subscribers per 100K.
Minimum sample
The next 2 tragedy-led Sundays. Three of the last seven Sundays led with a tragedy, so expect one or two in 28 days; the test may need to run longer.
Success threshold
Both at or above the typical band at 10:00, and APV of 34.6% (the new-cohort median) or more.
If it wins
Heavy weeks get a format: one case, six minutes, then a release beat.
If it loses
Heavy topics cost attention however they are cut. Keep covering them on news judgement, and protect those weeks with Experiment 2’s asks rather than structure.
